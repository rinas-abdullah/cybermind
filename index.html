The readiness gap

Workforces are growing. Readiness is still a guess.

Saudi Arabia's cybersecurity workforce passed 21,000 professionals in 2024. The harder question is whether they are ready for an attacker that adapts, and most training can't answer it.

01

Scripted scenarios

Trainees memorise one path. When the attacker changes plan, they lose the thread.

02

No pressure

Practice happens in calm rooms. Real incidents arrive with alerts, deadlines and a manager on the line.

03

One number

Pass/fail hides how someone thinks, so no one knows what they should train next.

04

Lessons lost

Incidents get a report and an archive folder. They rarely become training.

The Rasd loop

Five stations. Each one feeds the next.

What's new isn't any single feature. It's the connection: measurement becomes instructions for the adversary, and incidents become scenarios.

Measured gaps and new incidents flow back to the adversary, so the next exercise starts exactly where the team is weakest.

Controlled AI

The model proposes. The guardian decides.

An offensive AI has to be fully governed. In Rasd the language model has no power to execute anything. Every move is checked by a deterministic engine before it touches the simulation.

LLM

Language model

Proposes the next attacker move and explains why.

GUARDIAN ENGINE

Guardian engine

Validates against MITRE ATT&CK and the current simulation state. Anything outside is rejected. Every decision is explainable and replayable.

SIMULATION

Isolated simulation

Executes inside the platform only. Nothing ever reaches a real system.

Readiness, measured

From “we think we're ready” to a number you can defend.
